I’ve always been fascinated by technology as a way to navigate the natural world. As a kid, I devoured science fiction and dreamt of exploring the stars aboard faster‑than‑light ships. Later, my attention turned to technologies from a different era—wooden ships propelled by canvas sails—where we still sailed through the stars (but only on a clear night).

Today, that same curiosity drives my work with cutting‑edge AI. I build tools that help scientists and decision‑makers make sense of complex ecological systems—because I believe better models lead to better choices for our ocean planet.

Outside of research, I’m often on the water in a boat, or under it in a wetsuit.

## Short bio

For event organisers and introductions:

> Scott Spillias is a Research Scientist at CSIRO Environment in Hobart and a member of the Centre for Marine Socioecology at the University of Tasmania. He develops and tests AI methods for building marine ecosystem models, including tools that use large language models to find model structure, build food webs and screen scientific literature, and he is leading development of MOSAIC, a platform for building ecosystem models with an AI collaborator. He holds a PhD from the University of Queensland on the sustainability of seaweed farming, was a postdoctoral fellow at the International Institute for Applied Systems Analysis (IIASA), and is a National Councillor of the Australian Marine Sciences Association.
